A Note for Grown-Ups

This pack runs alongside the First Flake workbook, one kid page for every grown-up trip.

How it works

  • Do the workbook trips as usual. Each trip has a matching kid page in here: a game, a hunt, or a job to do.
  • After every trip, your junior prospector fills in a Junior Trip Log (page 12) and colors in a badge on the Badge Tracker (page 14).
  • Trip 7 ends with a certificate. Page 16 has two: one for the day they find a flake, one for finishing all seven trips. Not every family finds gold on the first try, so every kid still goes home proud.

Printing tips

  • Print the Junior Trip Log once per trip, and My Treasure Map as often as you like.
  • Print page 16 on card stock, then cut along the dashed line to get two certificates.
  • Print page 15 on full-sheet sticker paper, or on card stock and stick the badges on with tape.
  • Younger kids (5 to 7): you read and write, they draw, color and count. Older kids (8 to 12) can run their own logs.

Josie and Grandpa Ezra are the workbook's fictional guides, written with help from AI. Everything they teach is real. For personal and household use: print what your family needs, but please don't resell or share the file. hello@firstflake.com

The Junior Prospector
Safety Pledge

Real prospectors stay safe, so they can keep exploring.

I always pan with a grown-up, and I stay where my grown-up can see me.
I never go into the water alone. I stay in water below my knees.
I wear a life jacket near deep or fast water.
I don't climb on wet rocks or dig under creek banks.
I wear a hat, drink water, and take breaks when my hands get cold.
If I get lost, I stay put and blow my whistle three times.
I fill my holes and take my trash home. The creek is for everyone.

I promise to follow these rules on every trip.

How Gold Got Into the River

1. Long, long ago
Gold was locked inside rocks, high up in the mountains.
2. Rain, ice and time
Rain, ice and roots cracked the rocks into smaller and smaller pieces.
3. Floods move everything
Big floods carried sand, rocks and tiny bits of gold down into the creek.
4. Gold is SO heavy
Gold is about 7 times heavier than sand, so it sinks to the bottom and gets stuck.

The BB Rescue Challenge

Ten BBs are hiding in the gravel. Can you rescue all of them?

How to play

  1. A grown-up mixes 10 steel BBs into a pan of gravel.
  2. Shake the pan under the water, then tilt and wash, just like the workbook shows.
  3. Count the BBs left in your pan, and color in that many circles.
  4. Try to beat your own score every round!

Trip 5 · Black Sand School

Black Sand Detective

Where there's black sand, gold isn't far.

Swirl the pan. The black sand slides away and the gold shows its yellow tail.

The magnet experiment

  1. Put your magnet in a zip-top bag.
  2. Wave it just above the dry black sand in your pan.
  3. Watch the black sand jump up and stick!
  4. Gold never sticks. Whatever stays behind is worth a closer look.

Trips 5 to 7 · Real or fake?

Is It Gold? Junior Edition

Lots of things sparkle. Only one of them is gold.

Gold
the real deal
  • Bright yellow in sun AND shade
  • Soft: bends or dents when poked
  • Heavy: stays in the pan
Pyrite
"fool's gold"
  • Brassy, flashes in the sun
  • Hard and crumbly
  • Often shaped like little cubes
Mica
the sparkly trickster
  • Thin, shiny flakes
  • Looks dull from the side
  • Light: floats away easily

Who am I? Circle the answer.

1. It stays bright yellow, even in the shade.GoldPyriteMica
2. It flashes in the sun and crumbles when you poke it.GoldPyriteMica
3. It washes right out of the pan with the light sand.GoldPyriteMica
4. It's shaped like tiny boxes.GoldPyriteMica
5. It bends instead of breaking.GoldPyriteMica
6. It goes dull and silvery when you turn it.GoldPyriteMica

Answers: 1. Gold · 2. Pyrite · 3. Mica · 4. Pyrite · 5. Gold · 6. Mica

Grown-up note  The poke test uses a pin. Let a grown-up hold the pin, and never let kids bite or taste anything from the pan.
