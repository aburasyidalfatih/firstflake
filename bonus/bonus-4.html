The Trip 1 Paydirt Shortcut

Practice with real gold at your kitchen table before you ever drive to a creek. This is how you know your technique works, so when your pan comes up empty at the river, you know it's the spot and not your hands.

What paydirt is

Paydirt is real gold-bearing gravel, dug from a producing claim and sold in sealed bags. Most bags come with a guaranteed amount of gold. You pan it at home in a tub, exactly like Trip 1 in the workbook, except the "BBs" are real flakes.

It's not a way to make money. A bag usually costs more than the gold inside it. You're paying for practice, certainty, and the fun of seeing your first real color without leaving the house.

How to choose a bag

  • Guaranteed gold. Look for a stated minimum amount, and a seller with lots of reviews.
  • Start small. A sampler or 1–3 lb bag is plenty for your first sessions.
  • Real concentrate, not "salted" sand. Good bags come from actual claims and include black sand, which is perfect practice for Trip 5.
  • Know what's in it. Some bags add "pickers" (bigger flakes you can see). Fun, but fine gold is the real test of your technique.

The kitchen-table session (45 minutes)

  1. Set up the tub from Trip 1. Fill it with water, wide enough to sink your whole pan.
  2. Pour in a quarter of the bag. Don't do the whole bag at once.
  3. Run the Trip 1 steps: sink, break up clumps, shake hard under water, tilt riffles-away, wash, re-level, repeat.
  4. Stop when you're down to black sand. Switch to the Trip 5 finishing swirl and look for the yellow tail.
  5. Snuff every flake into your vial.
  6. Don't dump your tailings. Pan the leftover tub material a second time. Whatever gold you find there is gold you lost the first time. That's your real score.
  7. Write it on a log page: how many flakes in the first pass, how many recovered from the tailings.
Your target
When your second pass of the tailings finds nothing, your technique is river-ready. Go do Trip 2.
