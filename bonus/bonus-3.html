Is It Gold?

Three things in your pan glitter. Only one of them is gold. Print this page, cut out the card, and keep it in your kit with your vial.

Pocket ID card · cut along the dashed line

Gold glows, bends, and stays put.

GOLD

Shade: still glows warm yellow in your shadow
Pin: soft. Dents or bends, never shatters
Pan swirl: heavy. Stays at the bottom, barely moves
Shape: flat flakes, rounded edges
Magnet: no

MICA

Shade: goes dull or silvery in shadow
Pin: flakes apart in thin sheets
Pan swirl: light. Floats and washes away first
Shape: paper-thin, flashes in sun
Magnet: no

PYRITE

Shade: brassy, pale, goes dull
Pin: hard and brittle. Crushes to a dark powder
Pan swirl: medium. Moves with the black sand
Shape: sharp edges, often little cubes
Magnet: no (but often found with magnetite)

Weight check: gold is about 19.3 g/cm³, roughly 7× heavier than sand. If it moves easily, it isn't gold.

The three tests, step by step

1. The Shade Test (5 seconds)

Turn your back to the sun so your body shades the pan. Real gold keeps its warm, buttery color in the shade. Mica and pyrite need direct sunlight to sparkle and turn gray or pale in shadow.

2. The Pin Test (10 seconds)

Snuff the flake into your vial or onto a dry surface. Press it gently with the point of a sewing pin. Gold is soft and malleable: it dents, flattens, or bends. Pyrite is hard and brittle and crumbles. Mica splits into thinner sheets.

3. The Pan Test (30 seconds)

Put the flake back in a pan with a little water and black sand. Swirl slowly. Gold stays put while everything else drifts. Mica floats off almost immediately. Pyrite moves along with the black sand.

Other things that fool beginners

Looks like goldHow to tell
Weathered mica ("cat's gold")Golden-brown flakes that float and shatter under a pin
Brass or copper bitsUsually old trash: shell casings, wire. Green tint, sharp manufactured edges
ChalcopyriteBrassy with a greenish or iridescent tarnish. Brittle like pyrite
Wet yellow sandDry it. Real gold doesn't change color when dry
